Sustainability Readiness Check

Check how well your own public health intervention project can keep delivering benefits to your community after outside support ends, and start building the pieces of your sustainability narrative.

Transcript

Welcome. In this assignment you write a short narrative describing the approach you will build into your public health intervention project so that the project, and its benefits to your community, continue over the long term. You also describe the challenges you might face, and your strategies for overcoming them, and you support your plan with at least two credible sources from outside the required readings. This tool helps with part of that work. In step one, you describe your project and rate it on nine sustainability factors from the lecture, grouped into project design, the organization, and the community. In step two, you turn your weakest factors into challenges and write your own strategies. In step three, you record your outside sources and check how credible they are. In step four, you build an outline, draft your narrative, and watch the word count against the four hundred to five hundred word target. The tool does not write the narrative for you, and it does not cover everything in the assignment, so use it as a starting point. You can pause any audio on this page and resume where you left off.

Describe and rate. Describe your intervention project, then rate each sustainability factor for your project as it is planned today. You can rate only some factors and come back to the rest.

Transcript

Step one tip. Start by describing your project in your own words: its name, the community it serves, and what it does. Then rate each factor from one to five for your project as it is planned today, not as you hope it will be. A one means the factor is not in place at all, and a five means it is strong. Read the question under each factor and rate honestly, because your low ratings are what make the next steps useful. The factors fall into three areas from the lecture: project design and implementation, the organization that hosts the project, and the wider community. The chart on the right fills in as you rate, and the scores below it show how strong each area is. If you are unsure about a factor, leave it unrated and come back later. You can use the rest of the tool with only some factors rated.

Challenges and strategies. Factors you rated 2 or lower appear here as challenges. Write your own strategy for each one in terms of your project.

Transcript

Step two tip. Each card is a factor you rated two or lower. The left side describes the challenge in general terms, and the right side gives a hint from the lecture about how projects overcome it. Use the hint as a starting point only. In the box below, write your own strategy for your own project. Good strategies are specific: they say who will do what, and by when, and they fit your community. For example, instead of writing that you will find more funding, say which local organization could take over which cost, and in which year. If none of your factors are rated two or lower, the tool shows your two lowest ratings instead, because even a strong project has a weakest point. Strategies you write here will carry into your outline in step four.

Add sources. The assignment asks for at least two credible sources outside the required readings, based on similar projects. Record them here and check their credibility.

Transcript

Step three tip. The assignment asks for at least two credible sources beyond the required readings, and suggests two to three references. Look for sources about similar projects: a successful example, a project that struggled and what its authors suggest, or general advice on making projects sustainable. Good places to search are PubMed, Google Scholar, and the publications of the World Health Organization and national public health agencies. For each source, paste the citation in A P A style, choose the type of source, and tick only the credibility checks that are honestly true. Then write what the source shows about a similar project, and choose the factor it supports. If you cannot tick most of the checks, look for a stronger source. You can record up to three sources.

Draft. Build an outline from your own entries, then write your narrative of about 400 to 500 words in your own words.

Transcript

Step four tip. Press the build button to get an outline. It is a skeleton only: text in square brackets is a prompt for you to answer in your own words, and your own strategies and citations from the earlier steps are placed where they belong. The tool does not write the narrative for you. Your narrative should describe the approach you will build into your project to keep it going over the long term, describe the challenges you might face and your strategies for overcoming them, and cite your outside sources as evidence from similar projects. The word counter shows your progress toward four hundred to five hundred words. Words after a line that says References are not counted. The checks on the right show whether you have enough sources, and whether each challenge has a strategy. When you are done, copy the text, and check it once more against the assignment instructions.
