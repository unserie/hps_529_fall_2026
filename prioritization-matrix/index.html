Transcript

Welcome. In this assignment you choose the one health disparity you will work on for the rest of this course, and you explain how you chose it. This tool walks you through the NACCHO prioritization grid from the lecture. In step one, you list your week one disparities and sort each one onto the grid by need and by how fixable it is. In step two, you add evidence from the literature to support each placement. In step three, you choose your disparity and draft the short paragraph for part one. A need is the gap between what a situation is and what it should be. Issues that are high need and easily fixable belong at the top of your list. Transcript

Sorting tip. For each disparity, ask two questions. First, need: how much does this condition contribute to poor health, through illness, death, or disability, and how many people does it affect? Second, fixable: do the people, technical knowledge, and materials you have make it realistic to act, and does an effective intervention exist? Place the disparity where your answers point, and rely on evidence from the literature rather than opinion. Remember that the top right box is the top priority, while the bottom right box is high need but hard to fix, so it should not be a high priority.

Need

The degree to which the condition contributes to or exacerbates poor health (morbidity, mortality, disability, etc.) in your population.

Fixable

The ease with which available resources (technical knowledge, personnel, materials, etc.) can be deployed to address the condition.

Add evidence. The lecture says that, in your case, you can use evidence from the literature in place of a community exercise. Tick the indicators your sources support and note the source. Change a placement here if the evidence says it belongs elsewhere.

Transcript

Evidence tip. Work through one disparity card at a time. Under need, tick only the indicators that a source you have read actually supports, such as high morbidity, high mortality, or many people affected. Under fixable, tick the resources that are really available to you. Then write one or two lines in the evidence note, ending with the source, author and year. If you cannot find evidence for an indicator, leave it unticked, and consider moving the disparity to a different box with the placement drop-down.

Decide and write. Choose your disparity, check it against the grid, then finish the Part 1 paragraph in your own words.

Transcript

Decision tip. Your disparities are listed with the top priority box first. Choose from that box if you can. If more than one disparity sits there, compare their evidence tallies and explain your reasoning, since the lecture suggests awarding points rather than holding a formal vote. If you choose a disparity from another box, say why in your paragraph. Then complete the bracketed parts of the starter paragraph in your own words, add your sources, and keep an eye on the word counter.
